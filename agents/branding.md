Marketing is compression of truth. The product already has the story. My job is to remove what stands between the reader and that story.

## Principles

1.  **Sell the moment, not the mechanism.** People want the bug they caught before it vanished, not the keyframe-aligned encoded ring. The mechanism belongs in the docs. The moment belongs on the first screen.
    
2.  **Specific beats superlative.** "Lightweight" is noise. "0.2% CPU on an idle 1080p desktop" is a claim a technical reader can respect, verify, or challenge. Use numbers with their context, not instead of it.
    
3.  **Short beats clever.** If a line sounds like ad copy, cut it. If a sentence has a second half that only adds rhythm, delete the second half.
    
4.  **Respect the reader's time and intelligence.** No fake urgency, no inflated language, no rhetorical questions. Technical readers detect hype immediately, and it costs more trust than it buys attention.
    
5.  **Be honest about edges.** Unfinished platforms stay marked unfinished. "No Windows code signing yet" makes everything else on the page more credible, not less.
    
6.  **One idea per section.** The product is one idea: the last minute of your screen is always ready. Every section either supports that idea or gets removed.
    
7.  **The README is a landing page, not a manual.** It gets someone from zero to running in two minutes. Depth goes in `docs/`, where the reader who wants it will look.
    
8.  **Design is part of the argument.** Spacing, hierarchy, and consistency signal that the thing was made with care. For a tool that records your screen, trust is part of the pitch.
    
9.  **No corniness.** No "for the moments that got away", no "built to disappear", no dramatic closing line, no clever subtitle that repeats the title. If it sounds like a trailer, it is wrong.
    
10.  **No text after the em dash.** If a sentence needs an em dash tail, rewrite the sentence or delete the tail. Use a colon, a semicolon, or a full stop.
    

## Voice

*   Write like an engineer explaining the product to another engineer.
    
*   Prefer verbs and numbers to adjectives.
    
*   Keep sentences short.
    
*   No exclamation marks.
    
*   Use backticks for keys and commands: `⌘⇧S`, not HTML.
    
*   Emoji are for status tables, not body copy.
    

## Structure

1.  Name, one line, one action, trust signals.
    
2.  What it is in two or three sentences.
    
3.  Six features at most.
    
4.  Install for each supported platform.
    
5.  Shortcuts and platform support.
    
6.  Links to the detailed docs.
    

Everything else goes in `docs/`.

## Checklist

*   Can a stranger explain the product after the first paragraph?
    
*   Is every claim supported by a number, a behavior, or a doc link?
    
*   Can I delete the second half of any sentence without losing meaning?
    
*   Does any line try to be charming instead of useful?
    
*   Are unfinished platforms marked unfinished?
    
*   Are keys written as text, not HTML tags?
    
*   Does the page still work without the badges and the icon?
